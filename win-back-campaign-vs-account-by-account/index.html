Questions we get asked

Is a win-back email campaign enough to bring B2B customers back?

Not on its own. A campaign sends one message to the whole list, but B2B accounts leave for different reasons and each decision sits with a few named people. Account-by-account win-back works out who is worth a conversation at all, why they really left, whether that reason has expired and how your team should open the call.

What a win-back campaign does well

A win-back campaign is a sequence of emails sent to customers who have left, usually with news of what is new, a reason to look again and sometimes an offer. It is quick to set up and easy to measure. Where lost customers are many, small and similar, and they left for much the same reason, a campaign can be the right call.

It also does something useful by default: it reminds the whole list that you are still there.

The better campaigns are short and specific about what has changed. They lead with a real update, such as a feature that shipped or a plan that now fits, instead of a general note saying you miss them.

Why one message to the whole list falls short in B2B

B2B accounts do not leave for one shared reason. One left because the person who chose you moved on. Another because a feature was missing. A third because the budget was cut, and a fourth because onboarding stalled and nobody used the product. A single message cannot speak to all four, so it ends up speaking to none of them.

The decision also sits with a few named people. The person who signed may have gone. The person who holds the budget now may never have heard of you. An email to the old contact at the old company name is easy to ignore.

And some accounts should not get a message at all. Customers you terminated, accounts suspended for non-payment and anyone still paying you under another name all need to come off the list first. A campaign tends to go to everyone.

Timing is the other problem. A campaign goes out on your schedule. An account is only ready to listen when its own circumstances change: a new leader, a new budget, a new office. The same email that is ignored in March might have landed in June.

A campaign is a flyer through every door, and most end up in the bin.

What account-by-account win-back does instead

Account-by-account win-back starts from the other end. Instead of writing one message for everyone, it looks at each lost account and asks whether it is worth a conversation at all. For each account, that means checking:

  • whether you can contact them
  • who they are today, after any rename, merger or acquisition
  • why they really left, beyond the reason in the dropdown
  • whether that reason has since expired
  • who should take the call, and how to open it

What comes out is a short, ranked list of the accounts worth going back to, each with the reason it left, what has changed and why now. Instead of a flyer through every door, you know which door to knock on and what they will say. Our guide on how to win back lost B2B customers walks through each step.

Calls, not sequences

In B2B, the accounts worth winning back are usually worth a phone call. A call lets your rep acknowledge why the account left, show what has changed and listen. It is also much harder to ignore than another email.

A good call needs preparation. The rep should know the real reason the account left, what it has done since and which objections will come up. Before anyone calls, the pitch is tested against the people who will actually decide, such as the finance lead who watches the cost, the operations lead who remembers the rollout and the person who used the product every day. When your rep picks up the phone, the hard questions have already been asked and answered.

That preparation also keeps a win-back call from sounding like a sales script. The rep opens with what has changed for that account, so the conversation starts from something both sides recognise.

When to use each

Use a campaign when your lost customers are many, small and similar, and a single update is enough to bring some of them back. Use account-by-account win-back when each lost account is worth real money, the reasons for leaving vary and a badly timed message could close the door for good.

Many B2B teams do both: a light campaign to keep the wider list warm, and account-level work on the accounts that matter most. The mistake is assuming the campaign covers the second job. A useful test: if the email would read badly next to the real reason that account left, the account needs a call. Questions on this topic

Isn't this just a win-back email campaign?

A campaign sends one message to the whole list; Intellible works out, account by account, who is worth a conversation at all. We check who they are today, why they really left and whether that reason has since expired, then test the pitch on the people who'll decide. A campaign is a flyer through every door (most end up in the bin); we tell you which door to knock on and what they'll say.

Can a campaign and account-by-account win-back run together?

Yes. A campaign can keep the wider list warm while your team calls the accounts worth a conversation. Take the accounts you plan to call out of the campaign first, so they do not get a general email the week before.

Do you send the emails or make the calls?

No. Your team makes the call. We never contact your customers; we tell you who to call and how to open.

Who should make the win-back call?

Usually whoever the account is most likely to take a call from, which is not always the original account owner. For each account we name who to contact and how to open the call.
