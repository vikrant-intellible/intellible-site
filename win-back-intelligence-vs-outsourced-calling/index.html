Questions we get asked

Should we outsource win-back calls to an agency?

For the accounts that matter most, usually not. Your former customers remember your people, and a win-back call lands best from someone they trust. An agency adds calling capacity, but the hard part comes before the call: who to call, why now and how to open. Win-back intelligence does that research and leaves the call with you.

What agencies and outsourced sales teams do well

Win-back agencies and outsourced sales teams sell capacity. They bring trained callers, a script, a calling routine and someone to manage it, and they can start quickly. For cold outreach, or for a long list of small and similar accounts, that capacity can be worth paying for. You will also see them described as outsourced sales development reps, or SDRs.

Some go further and offer to run the whole effort for you. They take your list of former customers, call through it and pass back any account that wants to talk.

That is a fair offer when the person on the other end is a stranger. A former customer is a different call.

Why a former customer is a different call

A lost account already knows you. It remembers who sold to it, who ran the rollout and what went wrong. The opening question on any win-back call is some version of "why would it be different this time?", and the answer has to come from someone who can speak for the business.

That is hard to do on behalf of someone else. An outside caller can read the notes, but cannot say "we got that wrong, and here is what we changed" with the same weight as a person from your team. The account can tell the difference.

Who makes the call matters too. The right person is whoever the account is most likely to take a call from. Sometimes that is the original account owner, sometimes a senior leader, sometimes someone new who carries none of the history. It is rarely a name the account has never heard.

What you hand over when someone else makes the call

When you outsource win-back calls, you hand over more than a list of phone numbers.

  • The relationship: for the length of the call, someone outside your business speaks for you to people who once paid you.
  • The judgement on who to call: a team paid to make calls tends to make them. The accounts that should be left alone, such as the ones that left after a dispute, need someone willing to say no.
  • What is learned: the real reason an account left, and how it reacted to the call, is some of the most useful evidence you will get. It belongs with your team, in your CRM, rather than in someone else's call notes.
  • Your customer data: a full list of former customers, with contacts and history, leaves your business and sits with another company.

Agencies can do good work. The point is that a badly handled call costs more with a former customer than with a stranger, and the cost falls on you.

Where the real work sits: before the call

Most win-back efforts stall long before anyone picks up the phone. The hard part is deciding which lost accounts are worth a call at all, and that is research.

For each account it means checking whether you can contact them, who they are today after any rename or acquisition, why they really left, whether that reason has expired and who should take the call. Then the pitch is tested against the people who will decide, such as the finance lead who watches the cost and the person who used what you sell every day, until each likely objection has an answer.

Outsourcing the calls leaves this work undone. The caller ends up working from the same unranked list you had, at a faster pace.

Win-back intelligence does the research and hands the call back to you. What comes out is a short, ranked list your team can use on Monday morning.

When outsourcing makes sense, and when to keep the call

Outsourcing can make sense for a long tail of small, similar accounts, where each one is worth little on its own and a script covers most of what needs saying.

Keep the call with your team when each lost account is worth real money, the reasons for leaving vary and a badly timed call could close the door for good. That describes most B2B accounts worth winning back.

If your team is short of time, the answer is usually a shorter list. A ranked list of the accounts worth calling this week, each with an opening line and the likely objections answered, fits around existing work. If nobody owns lost accounts, nobody calls them, so name an owner either way. If you are weighing whether to run the research yourselves, read whether win-back research can be done in-house.

Questions on this topic

Does Intellible make the calls?

No, your team makes the call. We never contact your customers, and nothing goes out from us. We tell you who to call, why now and how to open.

Who should make the win-back call?

Usually whoever the account is most likely to take a call from, which is not always the original account owner. For each account we name who to contact and how to open the call.

Can we give the ranked list to an outsourced team?

The list is yours, so the choice is yours. Most of its value sits in the history behind each name, which is why the call tends to land best from someone the account already knows.

What if our team has no time to make the calls?

That is usually a sign the list has not been ranked. A ranked list is short: the accounts worth calling this week, each with a reason to call now, an opening line and answers to the likely objections. That fits around existing work.
