Win-back glossary: the terms we use

These are the terms we use when we talk about lost accounts and the work of deciding which ones are worth a second call. Each one is defined the way Intellible uses it across this site.

Win-back intelligence

The research that tells a B2B team which lost accounts are worth winning back, why each one really left, what has changed since and how to open the call. It works account by account and ends with a ranked list the team acts on.

Read more: what is win-back intelligence?

Churned account

A company that once paid you and has since left, whether it cancelled, let a renewal lapse or stopped using what it bought. Win-back intelligence works on churned accounts.

Lost prospect

A deal that never turned into a customer. It is a different list from churned accounts and needs different work, so it sits outside win-back intelligence.

Exclusion list

The accounts taken off the list before any work starts: blacklisted accounts, payment-suspended accounts, live customers and anyone else you name. It makes sure nobody you let go, or who still pays you, gets a win-back call.

Account resolution

Checking who a lost account is today, after any rename, merger or acquisition, so the call goes to the right company and the right person.

Reason for leaving

Why an account really left, worked out from what it said on the way out and how it actually used what it bought. It often differs from the reason picked in the CRM dropdown.

Read more: why lost B2B customers come back

Expired reason

A reason for leaving that no longer holds. The person who pushed to drop you has moved on, the missing feature has shipped or the budget has reset, so the account may be worth a call again.

Read more: how to tell when a reason has expired

Public signal

Something a company does in the open that bears on whether its reason for leaving still holds, such as a leadership change, a funding round, a launch, new hiring or a new office.

Read more: win-back intelligence vs intent data

Why now

The line that explains why this week is the right time to call a particular account, linking the reason it left to what has changed since.

Rehearsal

Testing the pitch before anyone calls, against modelled versions of the people who will decide, such as a finance lead who watches the cost. They push back one objection at a time until every concern has an answer worth saying out loud.

Read more: how the rehearsal works

Priority

How strongly an account is put forward for a call, shown as hot or warm. Hot means the reason has expired and the timing is right this week, warm means there is a credible reason to call with less urgency.

Ranked list

The finished output: the lost accounts worth winning back, in the order to call them, each with the reason it left, whether that reason still holds, what has changed, why now, who to contact and how to open the call.

Win-back call

A conversation your team opens with a churned account once there is a clear reason to talk again. It starts from what has changed for that account and is made by the person the account is most likely to listen to.

Read more: should you outsource win-back calls?

Win-back rate

The share of churned accounts that become paying customers again over a set period. Divide the accounts that came back in that period by the churned accounts you could have contacted.
