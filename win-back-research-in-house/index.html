Questions we get asked

Can we do win-back research in-house with our CRM or AI?

You can pull a list from your CRM, but a list is not the work. Every account needs checking: who they are today, why they really left, whether that reason has expired and who should take the call. With a short list, do it yourself this week. With a long one, it needs a real owner.

What your CRM can tell you

A CRM report is a good place to start. It tells you which accounts left, when, what they were worth and the reason someone picked from a dropdown. You can sort by value, filter by date and export the lot in minutes. Billing and product data add which renewals quietly failed and how usage faded week by week.

If your CRM is tidy, this takes an afternoon. If it is not, with missing reasons, duplicate records and accounts closed under the wrong owner, the clean-up alone can take longer than expected. Either way, you end up with a list.

That list answers the question "who left?". It does not answer "who would come back?"

What a list does not tell you

  • Who they are today. Companies rename, merge and get acquired. A CRM record does not update itself when the company it describes changes.
  • Why they really left. The dropdown reason is picked in a hurry and is often incomplete. If it says price, check their last login.
  • Whether that reason has expired. That depends on what has happened since, such as new leadership, funding, launches or hiring. None of it is in your CRM.
  • Who should take the call. The person who signed may have moved on. The person who holds the budget now may never have heard of you.

Answering these for one account takes time. Answering them for every lost account, and keeping the answers current, is the part that usually does not happen.

Each question also has a different source. Who they are today comes from company records and news. Why they left comes from your own emails, tickets and usage. What has changed comes from their public signals. Who to call draws on all of them. That is four kinds of research for every account on the list.

Can AI do the research for you?

AI can help. It can summarise notes, sort a long list and gather public information about a company quickly. Used well, it saves hours.

It also needs the right inputs. AI working from a thin CRM export gives thin answers, and it cannot read the support inbox or the call notes unless someone gathers them first.

What it cannot do on its own is judge. It will offer a reason even when the evidence is thin, and it will not know that the account in your CRM was acquired last spring unless someone checks. Win-back depends on getting the reason right and on being willing to say "we do not know". A confident wrong answer leads to the wrong call, or to a call that should never have been made.

Use AI to speed up the research. Someone still has to check each account, decide whether it is worth a call and stand behind that decision.

Why it usually does not happen in-house

Most teams have the skills to do this. What they lack is time and an owner. Sales is measured on new pipeline. Customer success is measured on the accounts it still has. Marketing runs campaigns rather than working individual accounts. Lost accounts sit between all three, so nobody is asked to look at them again. When someone is given the job, it usually sits on top of their existing work, so it slips the moment the quarter gets busy.

Doing nothing has a cost as well. Accounts whose reason for leaving has expired stay quiet, and when they are ready to buy again, they may not come back to you. A single email to the whole list feels like action, but it rarely does the account-level work; see whether a win-back email campaign is enough.

When to do it yourself, and when to bring us in

If you have lost twelve customers and remember exactly why each one walked, call them this afternoon. You do not need us for that. Our guide on how to win back lost B2B customers sets out the steps.

If the list is long enough that guessing has become expensive, the research needs someone whose job it is. That can be an internal owner with time set aside every week, or it can be us. If you keep it in-house, give the work a named owner, a fixed slot in the week and a simple format for each account: the reason it left, what has changed, why now, who to contact and how to open.

Questions on this topic

Can't we build this internally, or with our CRM or AI?

You can pull a list from your CRM, but a list is not the work. Every account needs checking: who they are today, why they really left, whether that reason has expired and who should take the call. That is research most teams never get to, and it is the work we do.

What data do you need, and how is it handled?

A read-only export of the accounts you have lost, in whatever state it's in (half-empty notes included). Send it over and we do the rest. Your data is used only for your work.

How do you know why they left if the notes are thin?

Notes are only one source. We check what your records say against the account's public signals since they left, such as leadership changes, funding, launches and hiring. Where we cannot back a reason, we say so rather than guess.

Who should own lost accounts?

Someone with time set aside to work them. If nobody owns lost accounts, nobody calls them. Decide who does.
