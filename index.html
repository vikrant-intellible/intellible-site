Win back lost revenue.

The customers you lost already know you, which makes them cheaper to win back than strangers; most companies just never ask twice.

Most companies can tell you how many customers left last quarter, but very few can tell you which of them would come back.

The answers are usually already there, spread across the CRM, the billing tool and a support inbox nobody rereads (which is a shame, because that is where the good stuff tends to be).

Who it's for.

For

Teams sitting on a pile of goodbyes

Revenue, customer success and growth teams at B2B companies with recurring revenue, enough lost accounts that guessing has become expensive, and a list nobody has opened since the last cancellation email.

Not for

Anyone who already knows every name

If you have lost twelve customers and remember exactly why each one walked, call them this afternoon (you don't need us for that). It's also not for anyone hoping to send the same win-back email to everyone.

Only in Intellible

The meeting before the meeting.

It starts with everything the account has already told you, from exit emails and support tickets to usage logs and the notes from the last call.

From that, it builds the people who will decide. A CFO who watches the cost, an ops lead who remembers the rollout and the person who used the product every day, each one an AI agent that thinks the way they do.

Then it hands them your pitch and lets them push back, one objection at a time, until every concern has an answer worth saying out loud.

So when your rep finally calls, the hard questions have already been asked (and answered). Most tools tell you who to call; this one tells you how the conversation will go.

What it will not do.

  1. Pitch someone who still pays you

    Live customers and anyone you have chosen to stop working with are kept off the list from the start.

  2. Invent a reason

    If it cannot back a reason with what the account said or did, it says it does not know.

  3. Send anything on its own

    Nothing goes out until a person has looked at it and agreed.

Questions

Before you
book a call.

Who is Intellible for?

B2B teams with recurring revenue and a pile of lost accounts nobody has opened since the last cancellation email; usually revenue, customer success or growth leaders.

What do I need to start?

A list of the accounts you've lost, in whatever state it's in (half-empty notes included). Upload it and the model does the rest.

What do I get back?

A ranked list of who's worth calling, and a file for each account: why they left, what has changed since, who to speak to and what to say when you do.

Isn't this just a win-back email campaign?

A campaign sends one message to the whole list; Intellible works out, account by account, who is worth a conversation at all. It checks who they are today, why they really left and whether that reason has since expired, then tests the pitch on the people who'll decide. A campaign is a flyer through every door (most end up in the bin); this tells you which door to knock on and what they'll say.

Does it contact my customers for me?

No; it tells you who to call and how to open, and the conversation stays yours.
